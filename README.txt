U.S. Senate Election Poll Analysis (1990–2016)
PROBLEM OVERVIEW
This repository presents a data driven analysis of U.S. Senate election polling data, collected every August from 1990 to 2016. The project focuses on evaluating and comparing the polling performance of Democrat and Republican parties across multiple dimensions. Specifically, the analysis explores:
 Geographic voting patterns across 49 U.S. states to identify regional political blocs.  
 Electoral cycle dynamics by examining polling results across the three Senate classes.  
 Longterm partisan trends over a 26year period to track shifts in voter sentiment.

The goal: Compare Democrat and Republican polling performance across:
* 49 U.S. states (identifying regional voting blocs)
* 3 Senate classes (understanding election cycle dynamics)
* 26 years of data (tracking partisan shifts over time)
PROBLEM STATEMENT
This analysis seeks to answer four key questions:
1. Polling Volume: How many Democrat and Republican polls were recorded across the 26year span?  
2. Electoral Performance Trends: How do Democrat and Republican election results compare year over year?  
3. State Level Preferences: Which states show the strongest polling support for Democrats and Republicans?  
4. Class Level Outcomes: Which party performs better in each of the three Senate classes
DATA SOURCE
The dataset used in this project is a publicly available Excel file containing 595 records across 11 columns. It was sourced from GitHub and serves as a real world case study for exploratory data analysis and visualization.
Tool Stack:  
 Microsoft Excel  
 Excel Pivot Tables  
 Excel Pivot Charts  
 METHODOLOGY
The analysis was conducted entirely in Excel, following a structured workflow:
 Data Cleaning:  
 All numerical fields were standardized to whole numbers to ensure consistency and accuracy during aggregation and comparison.
Exploratory Analysis:  
Data was summarized and transformed using Excel Pivot Tables to generate cross tabulations and summary statistics by state, year, and Senate class.
Visualization:  
Pivot Charts were created to visually represent trends, comparisons, and distributions, making the insights more accessible and interpretable.
KEY INSIGHTS


Democrat has the higher number of polls than the republican with 452 points. 



From the year 2002, polling volumes remained relatively low with a substantial increase starting from the year 2004 to 2010. The year 2016 has similar number of polls between the two parties. In the year 2010, republican has the highest number of polling units when compared with the remaining years. After 2010, the number decreased across the two parties and then increased in 2016. The smallest number of polls occurred in 1998.



Democrat election results reached an all-time high in 2006 with a value of 4,220 while lowest point in the dataset occurred in 1998 at 847. From 1990 to 2002, values remained relatively low ( between 847 and 1275). Starting in 2004 (2053), the overall baseline shifted significantly higher. The high years include 2006, 2010, 2016 and lowest years include 2008, 2012 and 2014.



For Republican, the overall peak occurred in 2010, with second-highest result occurred in 2016 while the lowest point in the year 1998. From 1990 to 2002, values remained relatively low and stable. Starting in 2004, the baseline values increased substantially. Notable periodic drops occurred in 2008 and across 2012-2014 before surging again in major election years.  

Democrat dominance occurred in 2006 with 4200 compared to approximately 3,400 for Republicans. Republican dominance occurs in 2010 and 2016. In the year 2010, republicans outperformed Democrats with 4,700 against 3900. Again, in 2016, republicans outnumbered Democrats with 4500 values against 3900. For the first decade and a half, both parties remained closely matched at low overall totals. 
Democrats held the lead in 2006,  2008 and 2012, while Republicans took the lead in 2010, 2014, and 2016. In the year 2010, both parties have the highest combined results.



Pennsylvania shows the largest total election results in the dataset for both parties, peaking above 1,800 with Democrats holding a slight edge over Republicans.
For Democrat Strongholds, Minnesota (MN), California (CA), and Wisconsin (WI) display significant Democrat leads, with Minnesota showing one of the largest gaps (~1,580 for Democrats vs. ~1,150 for Republicans).
While for Republican Strongholds, Florida (FL), Oklahoma (OK), Indiana (IN), Kentucky (KY), and Utah (UT) show clear Republican leads over Democrats.
Highly Competitive States include New Hampshire (NH), Minnesota's neighboring states like North Dakota (ND), and Wisconsin (WI) feature nearly equal values between Republican and Democrat results. While Hawaii (HI), North Dakota (ND), Vermont (VT), and Wyoming (WY) have the lowest overall recorded numbers, with totals staying below 200 for both parties.



Class 3 accounts for the largest share of results for both parties. 
Republicans hold a distinct advantage in Class 3, outperforming Democrats by 1,332 points. 
Democrats carry a clear advantage in Class 1, leading Republicans by 1,422 points.
Class 2 is extremely competitive, with Democrats holding a narrow lead of just 139 points over Republicans and represents the smallest share in the dataset. 

CONCLUSION
This analysis has answered the question listed in the problem statement.
Across the 26 years, republican has 25, 288 polling unit while Democrat has 25,740 with 452 points differences. In total democrat has shown higher election results than Republican and has assumed office in many times than the Republican. Democrat dominance cut across the year 1990 - 1992, 1996 -2000, 2004-2008, and 2012 while Republican lead in 1994, 2002, 2014 and 2016.  States that show the strongest polling support for Democrats include Minnesota (MN), California (CA), and Wisconsin (WI) while the states that show the strongest polling support for Republican include Florida (FL), Oklahoma (OK), Indiana (IN), Kentucky (KY), and Utah (UT). Republicans outperform democrats in class 3 while Democrats lead republican in Class 1.
